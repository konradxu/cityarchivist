The Jet d'Eau — a happy accident

It began in 1886 as a safety valve for the city's hydraulic power network. Today it throws 500 litres a second some 140 metres into the air — and became, almost by mistake, the symbol of Geneva.

Featured Hotel · Geneva

Beau-Rivage

The last of the family grand hotels.

Opened in 1865 and still owned by the fifth generation of the Mayer family, Beau-Rivage is the rarest thing in modern hospitality — a genuine grand hotel that never became a chain. Its heart is a five-storey painted atrium, lit from a glass roof, that has barely changed in a century and a half.

History happened here: the Empress Sissi of Austria died in one of its rooms in 1898; the treaty that recognised Turkey was drafted under its ceilings. Today the Chat-Botté holds a Michelin star, the terrace looks straight onto the Jet d'Eau, and the suites keep their antique furniture and lake-facing balconies.

Small, personal and quietly grand — the address Geneva keeps for itself.

Featured Hotel · Geneva

Four Seasons · des Bergues

Geneva's oldest grand hotel.

Opened in 1834 on the Quai des Bergues, this is the address that started Geneva's grand-hotel tradition — a Rive Droite façade looking across the water to the old town and the fountain.

Run today by Four Seasons: the lakeside Il Lago by Michelin-starred chefs, the Izumi Japanese rooftop with its terrace, and a spa tucked into the historic building.

The classic choice for the view back toward the Jet d'Eau.

Featured Hotel · Geneva

The Woodward

A Belle-Époque palace, reborn.

Behind a protected 1901 façade on the Quai Wilson, The Woodward opened in 2021 as an all-suite Auberge Resorts hotel — the most talked-about arrival on the lake in a generation.

Every one of its 26 suites faces the water; downstairs is a Pierre Gagnaire restaurant, and below that a two-floor Guerlain spa with an indoor pool.

Old bones, entirely new luxury.

Featured Hotel · Geneva

Hôtel d'Angleterre

The intimate one on the lake.

A few doors from Beau-Rivage on the Quai du Mont-Blanc, the d'Angleterre trades scale for warmth — 45 rooms with the feel of a private English house, all facing the water and the fountain.

The Leopard Bar is a Geneva evening in itself: live jazz most nights, a serious cocktail list and a fireplace for winter. Windows restaurant does the lake-view breakfast.

Small, personal, and perfectly placed.
